How the index works

Fully specified mathematical rules, no editorial overrides.

35%
Strength of record

70% national percentile in wins above benchmark; 30% national percentile in win percentage. The benchmark is the 25th-best team in our own results-only rating.

20%
Adjusted MOV

For each FBS opponent, adjust capped victory/defeat margin for home field and opponent strength. Average across completed games, then percentile rank it.

20%
Quality road wins

70% percentile in opponent-quality-weighted win credits (road multiplier already applied); 30% percentile in opponent-quality-weighted road wins alone.

25%
On-field efficiency

Actual play-level efficiency, sustained game control, explosive plays, discipline, turnovers, consistency, and red-zone execution, from public team-game datasets.

Inside the 25% on-field component

Each input becomes an FBS-wide national percentile, then receives its fixed share of the overall index.

Join on ESPN game ID and team ID; only games finalized in the results feed qualify. Data coverage determines how strongly measured efficiency is trusted; low coverage shrinks the efficiency component toward 50 without changing the 25% weight. No preseason or polls.

Step 1 · Establish independent opponent strength

Fit a regularized simple rating system using only FBS-vs-FBS final scores. For home team A versus away team B:

y = capped(home margin) − venue adjustment
minimize Σ[y − (RA − RB)]² + 3 × ΣRteam²

The home-field correction is 2.5 points for true home games, zero for neutral sites. Ratings are centered by regularization, so the average is near zero. Fixed preseason starting points are not used. Each team begins at zero and all ratings are recomputed from scratch.

FBS-to-FCS games are excluded from the opponent-rating fit so lopsided FCS results cannot inflate team strength. FCS games do count toward record and are handled conservatively elsewhere.

Step 2 · Limit blowout influence

Instead of treating a 63-point win as three times as impressive as a 21-point win, we cap its influence:

C(m) = sign(m) × [min(|m|, 14)
  + 0.5 × min(max(|m| − 14, 0), 14)]

Examples: 7-point margin → 7; 21 → 17.5; 28 → 21; 45 → 21. Losses are capped symmetrically. Venue is adjusted separately.

This rewards decisive wins while reducing incentives to run up scores against bad opponents.

Step 3 · Measure wins above benchmark

Construct a hypothetical team rated exactly at the 25th-highest strength rating among FBS schools. For each team on its actual schedule:

expected win probability = sigmoid(
(benchmark rating − opponent rating + venue edge) / 10
)

WAB = actual wins − Σ expected win probabilities

A road game imposes a 2.5-point disadvantage on the benchmark; a home game gives it a 2.5-point advantage. The 10-point logistic scale is a working calibration assumption, not a fitted betting-market probability.

FCS opponents receive a provisional fixed −25 strength rating for benchmark/SOS purposes. FCS wins add minimal résumé credit; FCS losses are appropriately costly.

Step 4 · Reward strength + road wins

Against FBS opponents, each victory receives quality credit that rises continuously from near-average opponents to the best teams:

q = clamp((opponent rating − FBS median + 3)
/ (25th-best rating − FBS median + 12), 0, 1.5)

quality credit = q × venue multiplier
home 1.00 · neutral 1.15 · true road 1.50

Aggregate quality win credit is combined 70/30 with a separately ranked weighted road-quality sum. Quality road wins count only true road wins against above-median rated FBS opponents.

The additional road bonus intentionally double-weights difficult road wins above their venue-adjusted WAB credit, reflecting the stated ranking philosophy.

Step 5 · Automatically load opponent-adjusted team-game statistics

Internally matched advanced datasets are converted to one row per game_id × team. Rows are matched to completed, eligible FBS-versus-FBS games only. For PPA, success rate and explosiveness, compare offense and defense against the opponent's other-game averages, excluding the direct matchup; stabilize that benchmark with two national-average pseudo-games.

Adjusted OFF = observed OFF + 0.5 × (national DEF-allowed mean − opposing DEF-allowed mean)
Adjusted DEF allowed = observed DEF + 0.5 × (national OFF mean − opposing OFF mean)
Net = mean(adjusted OFF − adjusted DEF allowed)

The 0.5 factor is a deliberately conservative adjustment, not a learned parameter. Only team-games with complete automatically loaded advanced statistics count as covered.

Step 6 · Score the 25% on-field bucket

Compute the eight national percentiles. Two-way consistency itself combines 60% positive-net-PPA game share and 40% low week-to-week PPA volatility. Game control, red-zone and penalty calculations are explicitly separate; PPA, success and explosiveness are adjusted for opponents.

Efficiency = 0.10 PPA%ile + 0.10 success%ile
+ 0.16 control%ile + 0.16 explosive%ile
+ 0.08 penalty%ile + 0.12 turnover%ile
+ 0.16 consistency + 0.12 red-zone%ile

The complete 35/20/20/25 weighting is used when at least 70% of expected FBS team-game rows have verified advanced measurements. For each team, on-field score = 50 + (observed on-field score − 50) × (covered FBS games ÷ FBS games played). No missing game is treated as a measured success or failure. The ADV COVERAGE column and each team's drilldown disclose exactly how much evidence supports the estimate. If total advanced-stat coverage falls below 70%, a clearly labeled résumé-only fallback is shown.

Step 7 · Normalize and rank

Every raw metric becomes a national 0–100 percentile (ties receive midranks). For a complete advanced file:

FULL CFMI = 0.35 × Strength of Record
+ 0.20 × Adjusted MOV
+ 0.20 × Quality / Road
+ 0.25 × On-field Efficiency

When advanced coverage is incomplete, observed on-field measurements are shrunk toward league-neutral 50 in proportion to each team's missing games. All four category weights remain fixed. If severe data loss reduces national coverage below 70%, the dashboard displays an explicitly labeled résumé-only fallback, computed as (0.35 × record + 0.20 × MOV + 0.20 × quality) ÷ 0.75. Ties use WAB, then name. Model parameters are uncalibrated hypotheses, not historical backtest results.

Data lineage & caveats

What is actually retrieved, and what is estimated by this model.

Public game-results data

SportsDataverse / cfbfastR 2026 game schedules CSV. Fields used: completed, start_date, home/away teams and divisions, neutral-site flag, conferences and final scores.

Each Sunday the publishing workflow downloads and validates 2026 game results, then publishes a cached snapshot. No account or paid API key is required. The data is served alongside this site to avoid browser restrictions. Weekly data coverage and the publishing timestamp are shown in the dashboard.

CollegeFootballData coverage documentation for drives, plays, PPA/success and other advanced endpoints. For public, analysis-ready ESPN enriched datasets, see SportsDataverse cfbfastR-cfb-data (its per-game advanced tables include EPA, penalty, turnover and situational measures). The weekly publishing workflow attempts the published advanced data releases and stores a local validated copy. Their publication can lag completed scores; the dashboard continues to rank teams with coverage-adjusted on-field evidence and shows the precise measured share for each team.

Important model limitations

The calculated rankings are original to CFMI, not an official poll and not calibrated against end-of-season outcomes. An FBS-only game network has uncertain relative strength between conferences early in the season. Rating shrinkage helps but does not eliminate uncertainty.

Opponent-adjusted offensive/defensive scoring diagnostics are displayed but receive no ranking weight. Without play-by-play, on-field PPA/success/control cannot honestly be computed. Quality and venue credits intentionally overlap with WAB, as an explicit philosophical choice. FCS opponent value (−25), venue edge (2.5), logistic scale (10) and ridge prior (3) are published adjustable assumptions, not verified empirical truths.
