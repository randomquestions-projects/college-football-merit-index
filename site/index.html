How CFMI V4 evaluates college football merit

Independent 2026-season results; no preseason opinions or conference-brand priors. All weights sum to 100%.

Model version 4.0.0
30%
Strength of record

85% wins above expectation versus a top-25-power benchmark; 15% win percentage. Harder schedules create fewer expected wins. Losses to strong opponents hurt less than losses to weak teams.

20%
Opponent-adjusted margin

Margin with diminishing returns, corrected for venue and the independently inferred power of each opponent. A strong road loss can be more impressive than a weak home win on this metric.

15%
Quality wins and road merit

Actual wins over better opponents earn more credit. Road victories receive a 1.52× multiplier, neutral-site wins 1.16×. FCS wins earn zero signature-win credit.

35%
Opponent-adjusted play quality

Eight measured categories including offensive/defensive EPA, success, explosiveness, consistency, drive-related proxy, finishing, turnovers and penalties. Every available metric is adjusted for opposition and sparse-data confidence.

Inside the 35% play-quality component

Overall CFMI weight, not just the share of the component. Missing measurements are never assumed to be high or low.

Step 1 — Derive opponent quality, not conference prestige

All teams start without a preseason advantage. For an FBS game, estimate a regularized margin rating by iterating over all opponents and opponents' opponents. Home advantage is 2.5 points; capped/diminishing margins prevent excessive blowout credit.

SRS(team) ≈ sum[adjusted margin - venue + opponent SRS] / (FBS games + 3.5)

We also run a season-only Elo sequence: all FBS teams begin at 1500; wins and upsets against strong opponents change ratings more than predictable victories over weak teams. A declining K-factor reduces volatility as evidence accumulates. Opponent power combines 78% margin/SRS and 22% season-only Elo signal.

Elo expected = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent Elo − team Elo − venue) / 400))
Change = adaptive K × (actual result − expected result) × capped margin factor

Elo is a secondary opponent-strength signal, not a separate fifth category or a preseason seeding mechanism. Opponent “top 25” in this model means top 25 by CFMI's own season-only power rating, never AP/CFP.

Step 2 — Evaluate every game in context

Harder wins raise wins-above-expectation, adjusted margin and signature-win credit. In a tough road loss, a close score can demonstrate strong play in adjusted margin, while the win/loss category still records a loss. A 30-point road win over an independently strong opponent is much more informative than the same margin against a weak FBS opponent. Published play-by-play efficiency inputs are now also filtered for garbage time.

Within signature-win quality, every FBS win has some positive credit, but the value grows smoothly with opponent strength and is multiplied by venue. FCS victories count in the W–L column but earn no quality-win points and are treated as weak evidence in adjusted margin.

Team résumé details expose the opponent's power rank, score, venue, a 0–100 performance-quality score, win quality and whether core advanced EPA data were available for the game.

Step 3 — Opponent-adjust each play metric

Use the same game's team statistics, but assess them relative to the opponent's performances in its other games. This leave-one-matchup-out baseline shrinks toward a league average with two pseudo-games, reducing circularity and noise. Adjust offense for opponent defense; adjust defense for opponent offense.

Adjusted offense = observed offense + 0.80 × (league-average opponent defense − opponent defense excluding this game)
Adjusted defense = observed defense + 0.80 × (league-average opponent offense − opponent offense excluding this game)

A strong EPA performance against an elite defense therefore earns more credit than the same raw number against a weak defense. A poor showing against a great offense is penalized less than the same showing against a bottom-tier offense.

Step 4 — Handle incomplete data honestly

Each of the eight on-field measures independently uses all valid observations; one missing finishing-drive or turnover field no longer discards the entire game. Every team/metric is shrunk toward neutral 50 based on the fraction of FBS games with that particular measurement.

Adjusted metric = 50 + (measured percentile − 50) × measured game fraction

Core EPA coverage must reach 70% nationally before CFMI activates its full opponent-adjusted model. Below that level, it clearly displays an emergency résumé-only fallback. Coverage indicators refer to both offensive and defensive EPA, not just quarter-score data.

Historical held-out check — 2024 and 2025

To test whether V4 generalizes, we recalculated weekly rankings using only previously completed games and evaluated the next week's FBS-versus-FBS winners. V4 correctly selected 69.2% of 481 held-out games in 2024 (V3: 66.1%) and 70.4% of 490 in 2025 (V3: 69.2%). The independent opponent-power signal alone reached 70.5% and 71.8%, respectively.

That distinction is intentional: CFMI evaluates earned results and quality of play, not solely who is most likely to win the next game. This is preliminary two-season validation, not proof of optimal weights or calibrated win probabilities. Review the full historical backtest, coverage and rank stability.

External inspirations: Massey's schedule-adjusted ratings, NCAA's Massey methodology explanation, FIDE's Elo rating updates, SP+ efficiency ratings and the FEI possession-efficiency framework. CFMI's formula and chosen parameters are its own and have not yet been historically calibrated. No AP/CFP rank enters the model.

Data lineage & caveats

What is actually retrieved, and what is estimated by this model.

Public game-results data

SportsDataverse / cfbfastR 2026 game schedules CSV. Fields used: completed, start_date, home/away teams and divisions, neutral-site flag, conferences and final scores.

Each Sunday the publishing workflow downloads and validates 2026 game results, then publishes a cached snapshot. No account or paid API key is required. The data is served alongside this site to avoid browser restrictions. Weekly data coverage and the publishing timestamp are shown in the dashboard.

CollegeFootballData coverage documentation for drives, plays, PPA/success and other advanced endpoints. For public, analysis-ready ESPN enriched datasets, see SportsDataverse cfbfastR-cfb-data (its per-game advanced tables include EPA, penalty, turnover and situational measures). The weekly publishing workflow loads the published 2026 play-by-play Parquet, builds neutral-state WP and actual scoring-opportunity summaries, and stores a small validated per-team-game file. It also retains the published advanced box-score data as fallback. The play-by-play feed can lag completed scores. Each week the site shows which team-games have measured play-by-play; if insufficient nationwide, it falls back to labeled box-score proxies while results still refresh.

Important model limitations

The calculated rankings are original to CFMI, not an official poll and not calibrated against end-of-season outcomes. An FBS-only game network has uncertain relative strength between conferences early in the season. Rating shrinkage helps but does not eliminate uncertainty.

Opponent-adjusted offensive/defensive scoring diagnostics are displayed but receive no ranking weight. The neutral-state win-probability model does not incorporate teams, conferences, Vegas spreads or AP/CFP; its out-of-sample 2024–25 Brier losses were 0.1365 and 0.1345. EPA/PPA, positive-EPA success, and yardage-based explosiveness are derived from observed non-garbage scrimmage plays. Control is calculated from win probabilities at each play within a possession, using a brand-neutral game-state model trained on 2023 only and validated on 2024 and 2025. The current résumé measures overlap somewhat; V4 reduces direct quality-credit weight to limit double-counting. Historical calibration is planned. FCS evidence policy, venue edge (2.5 points), Elo weights, expected-win logistic scale and SRS regularization are model assumptions, not empirically calibrated constants.
