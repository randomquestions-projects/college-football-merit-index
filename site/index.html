Earned merit. Measured strength.

Two core rankings, built for different questions. Merit Index evaluates which teams have earned the strongest résumé through their results and quality of competition. Power Index estimates which teams are strongest based on their on-field performance. The Matchup Predictor applies Power to individual games without changing either ranking.

Upcoming Week · CFMI Matchups

Merit measures earned résumé, Power measures neutral-field team strength, and Matchup Score predicts the winner of this particular game.

Matchup Score is a game-specific estimated win likelihood based on underlying neutral-field Power points, home-field advantage, and any historically validated rushing/passing offense-versus-defense interaction. Power and Merit rankings are not changed by a favorable matchup. Predictions are calibrated against earlier-season game outcomes when a validated parameter file is available; any probability is an estimate, not a guarantee. Betting spreads, injuries, live lineups and roster changes are not modeled.

M = earned Merit score · P = neutral-field Power index · X = opponent-specific Matchup Score (estimated win likelihood, 0–100%). The two teams' X scores total 100% for the game.

Why CFMI exists

Purpose & Methodology

The original purpose was to build a credible, independent, data-driven college football ranking system that evaluates teams based on what they've actually earned—not their reputation, conference, brand, or position in the preseason polls.

CFMI was born out of frustration with the perceived biases and inertia in traditional rankings like the AP and CFP polls. The goal is to see whether we can use comprehensive football data, rigorous statistics, and transparent methodology to produce something better.

TWO DISTINCT INDICES

Two questions. Two independent answers.

01ACTUAL ACCOMPLISHMENT

Merit Index

Who deserves to be ranked highest?

A ranking based on actual accomplishment, including:

  • Wins and losses: Results matter, but not every win is equal.
  • Strength of schedule: Beating Alabama should be worth significantly more than beating a weak opponent.
  • Quality wins: Reward teams that beat elite competition.
  • Head-to-head: Direct results should carry meaningful weight when comparing similarly accomplished teams.
  • Margin of victory: Convincing wins matter, especially against strong opponents. Running up the score against an FCS team shouldn't.
  • Venue: Winning on the road should be more impressive than winning at home.
  • Quality of losses: A narrow road loss to an elite team shouldn't be evaluated like a blowout loss to a weak team.

Merit is about what a team has accomplished, not what we think it might accomplish. It should provide a defensible answer to which teams have earned playoff consideration.

02PREDICTIVE STRENGTH

Power Index

Who is actually the strongest team?

The most deserving team isn't necessarily the strongest team.

For example, Utah might have a stronger résumé, while Alabama might be favored to beat Utah on a neutral field.

Power leverages more sophisticated statistics:

  • Opponent-adjusted offensive and defensive efficiency
  • EPA, success rate, explosiveness, scoring performance
  • Actual competitive strength of opponents
  • Early-season talent, returning production, and other reliable preseason evidence that fades as games are played
  • Evidence of how strong a team truly is—not merely its record

Power should estimate how good a team is today and how it would perform against other teams on a neutral field. The two indices should be genuinely distinct, not two differently weighted versions of the same metric.

THE STANDARD WE HOLD OURSELVES TO

Credibility and independence were always essential

The goal was never simply to create rankings that look reasonable or resemble ESPN, SP+, or the AP poll.

CFMI aims for independent credibility through:

  • Comprehensive, high-quality football data
  • Statistically defensible methodologies
  • Transparent calculations and weights
  • Consistent treatment of all teams
  • Historical analysis and validation
  • Clear explanations for why teams are ranked where they are

The results are publicly available through an interactive website that people can explore, scrutinize, and compare against established rankings.

SECONDARY APPLICATION

Matchup Predictor

Uses Power, game location and validated opponent-specific tendencies to estimate how a particular game might unfold. It does not change either national ranking.
